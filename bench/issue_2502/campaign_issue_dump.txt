## Premise test, measured: Qwen3.5-4B-Base's residual stream really is locally curved

Before fitting an overcomplete *manifold* dictionary it is worth establishing that there is curvature to find — otherwise a curved dictionary is a more expensive way to write a linear one. This is that measurement, and it needs no fit.

**Model.** `Qwen/Qwen3.5-4B-Base`, which is the newest open-weight model in its size family: the Qwen3.5 Small series (0.8B / 2B / 4B / 9B) shipped 2026-03-02, the later Qwen3.6 open-weight line is 27B-dense + 35B-A3B MoE only with no 4B member, and Alibaba's July 2026 releases (Qwen3.8-Max-Preview, Qwen-Audio-3.0-TTS, Qwen-Image-3.0) are API-only with no downloadable weights. Sources: [Qwen 3.5 family review](https://computertech.co/qwen-3-5-review/), [Qwen open-weights guide 2026](https://codersera.com/blog/qwen-3-5-complete-guide-2026/), [Qwen3.6-35B-A3B](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.6-35B-A3B), [open-weight retreat](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/qwen-closed-flagship-pivot-open-weight-retreat-2026).

**Data.** 600,064 residual-stream rows (2560-d, fp16) at layers 8 / 16 / 22, wikitext-103-raw-v1, seq-len 512. 30,000 rows per layer sampled for this measurement, reduced to a 128-d PCA chart.

### What is measured

For each of 48 deterministic farthest-point landmarks per layer, take its `m` nearest neighbours and split them in half:

1. on the **fit half**, estimate the local centre and the top-`d` local PCA tangent frame;
2. project the **held-out half** into that frame, splitting each row into `d` tangent coordinates and the normal remainder;
3. regress the normal remainder on **quadratic** monomials of the tangent coordinates, with coefficients fit on the fit half only;
4. score the held-out R² of that quadratic normal fit.

This is the out-of-sample explanatory power of the second fundamental form. A locally flat patch plus any amount of isotropic noise scores **zero in expectation**, because the quadratic is never scored on rows it was fit on.

### The control that makes the number mean something

A high-dimensional cloud can look curved from concentration alone, so the number is priced against a **Gaussian with the same covariance**, run through the byte-identical pipeline. A Gaussian is locally flat by construction, so its score is this statistic's own finite-sample floor — and that floor is *below* zero, because the quadratic overfits and is punished out of sample. The real arm has to beat the floor, not beat zero.

![local curvature census](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SauersML/gam/5e8fafcf5825c85e2b80e41ef042bf44b7f085d3/artifacts/issue_2502_curvature_census.png)

### Result

**27 of 27 cells** (3 layers × 3 neighbourhood sizes × 3 tangent dims) put the real activations above the Gaussian floor. Minimum Welch t over all cells = **2.2**; at neighbourhood 512 the separation is 7–14 sigma.

Full 27-row table with standard errors: `artifacts/issue_2502_curvature_census_stats.jsonl`.

Two structural facts fall out of the sweep:

- **Curvature grows with neighbourhood radius** (128 → 512 monotonically, every layer, every `d`). That is the signature of real extrinsic bending rather than a noise artifact: a wider patch subtends more of the curve, while a noise artifact would wash out as the patch grows.
- **At the smallest radius the real arm can go negative** (L16, m=128, d=4 scores −0.042) **and still beat its null** (−0.054). At 64 fit rows against 15 quadratic parameters the estimator is starved; what survives is the *excess*, which is why the Gaussian control is load-bearing and not decoration. Reading the real arm alone against zero would have called this cell flat.

### What this does and does not license

It licenses curved atoms: local neighbourhoods bend away from their own tangent plane in a way a flat model provably cannot capture, at every depth probed. It does **not** yet say the curvature is *dictionary*-shaped — that a small number of reusable charts explains it — which is what the overcomplete fit has to earn, and it does not say curved atoms win on held-out reconstruction at matched sparsity. Those are the next two measurements.

## The declared split for this campaign — and what the old one was worth

Every arm of this campaign (mine and the parallel sessions') has to train and score against
**one** split, or the arms are unrelated numbers rather than a comparison. This declares that
split, and — because a contract asserted without a price is just an opinion — measures what the
split we had been using was actually costing.

### The split

The harvest is 600,064 token rows drawn from **5,774 wikitext documents**, a mean of 104 rows per
document. The prep in use until now cut those rows with `rng.permutation`, so tokens of the same
document — often adjacent positions of one sentence — sat on both sides of the held-out boundary.

The boundary now follows the document, assigned by a keyed hash of the document id:

```python
from artifacts.issue_2502_doc_split import row_side, split_manifest
held_out = row_side(np.load("harvest/doc_ids.npy"))   # boolean mask over rows
```

No RNG, no seed ordering, no shuffle implementation: identical across machines, Python versions
and numpy versions, stable when rows are subsampled or reordered, and the same for layers 8/16/22
since they share one row ordering. `split_manifest` binds the assignment to the harvest it came
from via a digest of the doc-id column, so quoting the hash quotes both the rule and the data.

### What the row split was worth

Both arms are byte-identical except which rows are held out: same harvest, same pool sizes, same
sink peel, same norm guard, same train-only PCA-128 chart, same models, same seeds. The row arm
reproduces the campaign's existing numbers to three decimals (PCA-8 `0.3249` against the posted

(held-out explained variance in the ambient 2560-d residual stream, so the comparison does not

**The leak is real, it is confined to the estimator with the capacity to exploit it, and it is

warning was larger than the effect, and I withdraw it.

### Why it is small — the mechanism, not a shrug

Only **5.4%** of held-out rows under the row split had their nearest training row drawn from their
own document. And the median distance from a held-out row to its nearest training row is **62.1%**
of the RMS row norm under the row split against **62.7%** under the document split — essentially
unchanged. At 50,000 training rows the residual stream is dense enough that document identity
barely determines proximity: a row from an unseen document is about as close to the training set
as a row from a seen one. That is why removing the contamination moves the score so little, and it
also predicts the effect would grow at smaller training-set sizes, where document identity would
carry more of the proximity.

The contract still stands regardless of its price — it is a correctness guarantee that costs
nothing to hold, and the 0.32 points it removes from the flagship comparison are exactly the
points that would otherwise be argued about.

## The wall: why no support-sparse manifold fit on this harvest has ever finished

Every `sae_manifold_fit` attempt on the Qwen3.5-4B harvest — mine and the parallel session's,
across `p=128` and `p=512`, `K=512` through `K=1024` — dies the same way:

```
SaeSupportSparseTerm::solve_fixed_point did not recur within N cycles (raw KKT max=...)
```

where `N` is exactly the `n_iter` the caller passed. That coincidence is the first clue, and it
turns out to be three separate problems wearing one error message. **All three are at current
main**: `support_term.rs`, `support_outer.rs` and `support_sparse_sae_ffi.rs` are blob-identical
between wheel 0.1.259 and `HEAD`, so nothing here is stale-wheel behaviour.

![inner stall](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SauersML/gam/73cf09f0f0c8b7360c50497b4bbfec2cb6bab53c/artifacts/issue_2502_inner_stall.png)

### 1. One knob is driving two budgets

`crates/gam-pyffi/src/manifold/support_sparse_sae_ffi.rs:411-412`:

```rust
max_outer_iter: request.max_iter,
max_inner_iter: request.max_iter,
```

`request.max_iter` is python's `n_iter`, documented and used everywhere else as the **outer**
REML smoothing-search budget. It is also, silently, the budget for the **inner** alternating
decoder/coordinate fixed point — a completely different quantity in different units. Since
`solve_fixed_point` requires two *consecutive* candidate cycles before it may report success,
`n_iter=2` cannot succeed unless the very first cycle lands exactly on the fixed point.

The comparison that makes this a defect rather than a choice: `gam-sae/src/tiered/fit.rs:29`
documents Tier-2 as driving `run_sae_support_outer` "**driven exactly as the public support-sparse
fit entry drives it**", and `Tier2SupportConfig::default()` gives it `max_outer_iter: 64` and
`max_inner_iter: 256`. The two drivers of one engine disagree 4:1, and the public one cannot
express the tiered one's setting at all. (This is the [parity-comment pattern](https://github.com/SauersML/gam/issues/2502) again: a comment asserting two paths are the same is a marker that they have diverged.)

### 2. But the budget is not what is stopping the fit

I bounded the fix before writing it. Laddering `n_iter` with everything else held fixed
(4000×64 chart, K=128 circle atoms, top_k=4, `gpu="off"`):

| n_iter | 2 | 4 | 8 | 16 | 32 | 64 | 128 | 256 | 512 | 1024 | 2048 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| raw KKT | 116.5 | 40.6 | 18.6 | 11.5 | 10.8 | 5.35 | 2.57 | **1.38** | **1.43** | **1.91** | **1.34** |

It descends to ≈1.4 by 256 cycles and then **stops** — eight times the budget from 256 to 2048
buys nothing. The tolerance is `1e-6`. The plateau is roughly **twelve orders of magnitude above
the round-off floor** for this problem (≈5e-12), so this is a real stall at a non-stationary
point, not an iteration count that merely needs raising. **Fixing (1) alone would not have made a
single fit converge.**

The other obvious suspect is dead too: the inner coordinate step is a trust-region Gauss-Newton
step capped by `trust_radius`, which the FFI sets from python's `learning_rate`. Re-running the
ladder at `learning_rate` 1.0 and 4.0 reproduces the default arm **value for value** (18.58 /
10.81 / 2.571 at n_iter 8 / 32 / 128), and only 0.2 is worse. The step cap is not binding.

### 3. And the bar it is being judged against is in the wrong currency

`solve_fixed_point` accepts when `raw_stationarity().max_abs() <= tolerance`, with `tolerance`
hardcoded to `1e-6` at the FFI boundary. But look at what that residual is
(`support_term.rs:1078-1094`): the decoder block accumulates `phi[basis] * residual[row, output]`
**summed over every row the atom serves**. It therefore carries the units of the data and grows
with the row count. An absolute constant is being compared against a quantity with neither
property.

Measured, at fixed `n_iter=32`, changing *only* a multiplicative constant on the activations:

| RMS row norm | 0.061 | 0.611 | 6.108 | 61.08 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| raw KKT at termination | 3.2e-3 | 3.83 | 10.8 | 5.2e2 |

**The same model, on the same rows, judged five orders of magnitude differently depending on the
units the activations were stored in.** Row count moves it too (500 → 8,000 rows takes the
residual from 6.7 to 12.4 at fixed scale). At the natural scale of a PCA chart of this residual
stream the bar is unreachable by construction — which is why a fit that reaches EV ≈ 0.5 still
reports failure.

### What I am not claiming

That (3) alone explains it. Even normalised by data scale and row count, the plateau at 1.4 sits
around 5.7e-5 — still fifty-fold above `1e-6`. So a relative criterion would make the diagnostic
*interpretable* but would not by itself pass this fit. **(2) is the substantive wall** and needs
its own root cause: why does exact-block decoder descent alternating with per-row trust-region
Gauss-Newton stop descending at a point that is not stationary? My leading hypothesis is the
minimum-norm decoder solve (`solve_psd_minimum_norm`, `decoder_sweep:824`) leaving the component
of the right-hand side outside the range of a rank-deficient atom Gram permanently in the
gradient — atoms serving fewer rows than their basis size would do exactly this, and the fix
would be structural rather than numerical. That is the next measurement, and it needs a Rust
build to instrument the per-atom row census.

Reproduce: `artifacts/issue_2502_inner_budget_ladder.py`, `artifacts/issue_2502_inner_kkt_scale.py`,
figure `artifacts/issue_2502_inner_stall_plot.py`, raw records
`artifacts/issue_2502_inner_stall.jsonl`, all landed in `73cf09f0f`.

**Practical note for anyone else fitting on this harvest: do not spend compute raising `n_iter`.**
It is measured not to help past 256.

## The curvature is real and it is *local*: it does not transfer between landmarks

The census earlier on this issue showed neighbourhoods bend away from their own tangent plane
beyond a covariance-matched Gaussian. That licenses curved atoms. It does not establish what a
*dictionary* needs — that a small number of reusable shapes explains the bending. Since the
support-sparse fit is blocked on #2517, here is that stronger question asked directly, with
nothing fitted. Landed `356fdab57`.

**Design.** At each of 24 farthest-point landmarks: fit a local tangent frame and a quadratic
model of the normal excursion on that landmark's own training rows; score it on held-out rows of
the same neighbourhood; then carry it to every other landmark under the orthogonal map that best
aligns the two tangent frames (Procrustes) and score it there. Rows come from the declared
document split (`split_hash` `6e3dbf8dcb8164bebc9d58eaacb56067`). Two design points that
determine whether the numbers mean anything:

- each neighbourhood's rows are cut **at random, not by radius** — an inner-half/outer-half cut
  makes every model extrapolate to a radius regime it never saw, which is a property of the split
  rather than of the manifold (my first run did exactly that and scored −0.56 on its *own*
  neighbourhood);
- the target is the **magnitude** of the normal excursion, not the normal vector. A normal vector
  lives in the neighbourhood's own (D−d)-dimensional normal space, and those differ between
  landmarks, so carrying a vector-valued model would score A's normal directions against B's and
  measure frame mismatch instead of shape.

**Results** (held-out R², 24 landmarks, 552 ordered landmark pairs):

| arm | m | d | own neighbourhood | transferred | **random rotation** | best transferred pair | best random pair |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| real | 256 | 2 | **+0.350 ± 0.036** | −2.812 ± 0.214 | −2.836 ± 0.234 | +0.553 | +0.584 |
| real | 256 | 3 | **+0.421 ± 0.038** | −2.921 ± 0.179 | −3.004 ± 0.199 | +0.460 | +0.488 |
| real | 512 | 2 | **+0.398 ± 0.038** | −1.734 ± 0.118 | −1.722 ± 0.126 | +0.711 | +0.651 |
| real | 512 | 3 | **+0.492 ± 0.034** | −1.907 ± 0.123 | −1.829 ± 0.129 | +0.672 | +0.521 |
| gaussian | 256 | 2 | −0.044 ± 0.009 | −0.127 | −0.126 | +0.061 | +0.068 |
| gaussian | 256 | 3 | −0.073 ± 0.011 | −0.167 | −0.164 | +0.048 | +0.060 |
| gaussian | 512 | 2 | −0.010 ± 0.004 | −0.081 | −0.080 | +0.030 | +0.029 |
| gaussian | 512 | 3 | −0.020 ± 0.006 | −0.097 | −0.096 | +0.031 | +0.023 |

**The local model is real.** It explains 35–49% of the held-out excursion in its own
neighbourhood, while the covariance-matched Gaussian — locally flat by construction, run through
the byte-identical pipeline — scores *negative* everywhere. That reproduces the census conclusion
through a completely different statistic, which is worth having on its own.

**The transfer is not.** Carried to another landmark, the aligned model is **statistically
indistinguishable from a random orthogonal map** — in the mean, in the best of 552 pairs, and in
the fraction of pairs clearing zero (7–14%, and the same either way). Procrustes alignment of the
tangent frames carries no information about the shape. Whatever bends each neighbourhood, it is
not one shape wearing different rotations.

### What this does and does not license

It does **not** refute a dictionary, and I want to be precise about why. Farthest-point landmarks
are maximally separated *by construction* — the least likely pairs to share an atom — and an
overcomplete dictionary explicitly allows different neighbourhoods to draw on different atoms.
K=32,000 atoms over 24 mutually-distant landmarks predicts almost no pairwise sharing.

What it does close off is the **shortcut**: there is no universal second fundamental form to be
read off the data, so "the curvature is dictionary-shaped" cannot be established without actually
fitting the dictionary and showing atoms are reused. The remaining honest version of the
overcompleteness claim is an alive-atom census with per-atom row counts from a converged fit —
which is exactly what #2517 is blocking.

## Progress update: routing fixed and landed, benchmark gap narrowed but not yet closed

### What was actually wrong

The lane was not underperforming because of the model class. It was underperforming because rows were routed to atoms by a **hash**, and I found four compounding defects in that path (detail in #2577):

1. `reroute_fixed_decoder` drew each atom's chart coordinate from `support_seed::projection` — a CountSketch hash of the row bits — then scored the atom at that arbitrary point.
2. Cold-start `score` is `hypot` of two hashes and never reads the decoder at all, so initial assignment was effectively random.
3. Selection was marginal top-`s`. With `K > P` the dictionary is necessarily coherent (Welch), so the `s` best-individually atoms are near-duplicates spanning far less than the `s` best jointly.
4. The greedy score omitted the ARD coordinate prior that `penalized_objective` charges — so routing maximised a *different functional* than the acceptance test evaluated.

Defect 4 is the one I'd flag as generally instructive. The guarded support move accepts only on a strict decrease of the full penalized objective, but routing was scoring the fit term alone. The prior is charged on `n × support_k × axes` slots, so the omission grows with K while the per-atom fit gain does not — which is exactly what the telemetry showed:

| K | support moves accepted | held-out EV |
|---|---|---|
| 2,000 | 1 of 3 | 0.8206 |
| 4,000 | 1 of 3 | 0.8290 |
| 8,000 | **0 of 3** | 0.8039 |

A guard that rejects *more* as the model grows is evidence the proposal is scoring the wrong functional, not evidence the model is saturating.

measure(#2502): the residual streams curvature is real and local, and does not transfer between landmarks

The curvature census established that neighbourhoods bend away from their own
tangent plane beyond a covariance-matched Gaussian. That licenses curved atoms. It
does not establish the stronger claim a dictionary needs -- that a small number of

allows different neighbourhoods to draw on different atoms, so a shared dictionary is

## ISSUE #2577

TITLE : Overcomplete SAE routes atoms by a hashed coordinate, not the atom's best coordinate — selection is near-uncorrelated with which curved atoms can represent the row, and held-out EV degrades as K grows
STATE : OPEN
AUTHOR: SauersML
OPENED: 2026-07-27T15:45:21Z
UPDATED: 2026-07-27T18:33:28Z
URL   : https://github.com/SauersML/gam/issues/2577
LABELS: 

-------------------------------- ISSUE BODY ------------------------------------
Atom selection in the overcomplete support lane scores every candidate atom at a **hash-determined
coordinate** instead of at the coordinate that atom would actually use. That defeats the point of a
curved atom, and it is why the lane's held-out reconstruction collapses relative to its training fit.

### The code

`SaeSupportSparseTerm::reroute_fixed_decoder` (`crates/gam-sae/src/manifold/support_term.rs:281`),
which is the out-of-sample path behind `ManifoldSAE.reconstruct` / `converged_latents`:

```rust
let candidate_coords = (0..atom.latent_dim())
    .map(|axis| {
        let raw = super::support_seed::projection(row_values, atom_index, axis + 1, random_state);
        super::support_seed::chart_coordinate(atom.basis_kind(), axis, raw)
    })
    .collect::<Vec<_>>();
...
let (phi, _) = evaluator.evaluate(coordinate.view())?;
let decoded = phi.row(0).dot(&atom.decoder_coefficients);
let score = row.iter().zip(decoded.iter())
    .map(|(truth, fit)| 2.0 * truth * fit - fit * fit)
    .sum::<f64>();
```

The **score is right** — `2·x·γ − γ·γ` is the exact squared-error reduction from adding that atom, a
proper greedy/matching-pursuit criterion, and it correctly uses the trained decoder. The **coordinate
is wrong**: `support_seed::projection` is the bounded-work CountSketch hash (8 samples, a deterministic
function of the row and the atom index). So each atom is judged at an essentially arbitrary point on
its own curve.

An atom whose curve passes very close to this row *somewhere* is rejected because its hashed point is
far away; an atom that hashes to a lucky point wins. Selection is therefore close to
uncorrelated with which atoms can actually represent the row. `solve_coordinates_fixed_decoder` then
optimises the coordinates properly — but only over the `top_k` atoms that were already chosen using
un-optimised ones.

### Why it shows up out of sample and not in training

At fit time the same hashed routing runs at the seed, and then the decoders are fitted *to whatever
routing came out*. The dictionary co-adapts to its own arbitrary assignment, so training reconstruction
looks fine. At inference the decoders are frozen, the routing is re-derived from hashes on new rows,
and nothing can compensate. Measured on Qwen3.5-4B-Base L16 (PCA-128 chart, doc split, `top_k=8`,
fixed λ=1, all fits inner-certified):

| K | train EV | held-out EV |
|---|---|---|
| 2,000 | 0.7295 | 0.5449 |
| 4,000 | 0.7838 | 0.5008 |
| 32,000 | 0.9313 | (collapses) |

Held-out EV gets **worse as K grows** even though training EV rises to 0.93 — the more atoms there are,
the more likely a badly-hashed-but-well-suited atom is displaced by a luckily-hashed one. A plain TopK
SAE on the same data and the same L0 reaches 0.7399 held out. A curved dictionary carries strictly more
parameters per atom than a linear one; losing to it is the symptom that pointed here.

### Fix

Score each candidate atom at **its own best coordinate**, not at a hash:

- `d_atom = 1` (the whole `auto` portfolio today: linear / euclidean / periodic) makes this a 1-D
  maximisation of `2·x·γ_k(t) − |γ_k(t)|²` per atom.
- It vectorises: precompute `Γ_k` on a shared grid of `G` points per atom once per fit, and the whole
  selection becomes one `X · Γᵀ` GEMM (`n×p` by `p×KG`) followed by a max over `G` and a top-`k` over
  `K`. That is the same shape as the dense-SAE encode the GPU lane already handles, and it removes the
  per-row per-atom `evaluator.evaluate` call that dominates the current routing.
- Cheaper interim: keep the hashed point as the initial guess and take a few Newton/golden-section
  steps on `t` before scoring.

The same defect exists at seed time in `support_seed.rs` (`score()` is `projection(...).hypot(projection(...))`,
a pure hash with no reference to any decoder), which is defensible for a cold start where no decoder
exists yet — but it means the fit never gets a routing that reflects the atoms it learned, at any point
in its life.

Found while benchmarking gam#2502; this is that issue's blocker, not the LAML cost in #2576.

evaluating at `support_seed::projection`'s hash. 17-point grid, everything else identical.

**+0.0505 EV, +9% relative.** Train EV is bit-identical between the two arms, which is the control

a held-out row.

### The larger finding this exposed

`solve_fixed_point` never re-routes. Its loop is exactly

```rust
let decoder_change = self.decoder_sweep(target, lambda_smooth)?;
let ... = self.coordinate_sweep(target, ard_precisions, trust_radius, tolerance)?;
```

so the support assignment is fixed at the cold-start seed — a pure CountSketch hash chosen **before
any dictionary exists** (`support_seed::score` is `projection(..).hypot(projection(..))`, with no
reference to a decoder, which is unavoidable at a cold start) — and is never revisited for the life of
the fit. Decoders and coordinates then spend hundreds of cycles co-adapting to an assignment that was
made at random.

That is the structural difference from the baselines this lane is measured against: a TopK SAE
re-selects its top-`k` latents on **every forward pass** through its learned encoder, so its assignment
co-evolves with its dictionary. Here the atoms never get to claim the rows they would best explain.

the current decoders every N cycles, using the corrected best-coordinate score. I have that implemented

## The accept/reject telemetry settles the design: **one** guarded move, and its value is gated by rows-per-atom

Full logs from the guarded runs, which turn out to explain the K-curve exactly:

```
K=2,000  cadence 25:  accepted @26            rejected @51                 -> 67 cyc, EV 0.8206
K=2,000  cadence 15:  accepted @16            rejected @31, @46            -> 57 cyc, EV 0.8206
K=4,000  cadence 25:  accepted @26            rejected @51                 -> 71 cyc, EV 0.8290
K=8,000  cadence 25:  rejected @26, @51, @76  (NONE accepted)              -> 100 cyc, EV 0.8039
```

Three conclusions, all of which simplify the fix:

**1. Exactly one move is ever accepted, and it is always the first.** After that the support is
locally optimal for the learned decoders and every further proposal is rejected. So the design
is *one guarded support move*, not a periodic re-route — which removes the cadence knob
entirely and makes the whole thing nearly free.

**2. Cadence is irrelevant.** 15 and 25 give **identical** held-out EV (0.8206) at K=2,000; the
only difference is when the single accepted move happens (cycle 16 vs 26) and hence the total
cycle count (57 vs 67). Nothing is gained by trying more often.

**3. K=8,000's underperformance is explained: it never got the fix.** All three of its moves
were rejected, so that arm is a routing-only fit with no support correction. The mechanism is
that the move is *proposed using the current decoders* — at K=8,000 there are `n·s/K = 45` rows
per atom, the per-atom decoders are correspondingly noisy, and a proposal scored against noisy
decoders does not beat the incumbent. **The support move's value is gated by rows-per-atom, the
same quantity that sets the conditioning floor** (`K ≲ n·s/10`, measured earlier: at
`n·s/K = 2` the Gram is rank-deficient and the fit refuses outright).

That also predicts the fix for the residual K-curvature, and it is not in this issue: the
proposal quality is limited by decoder quality, which is limited by the *seed* — the one stage
that still partitions rows by a decoder-free hash (`support_seed::score` is
`projection(..).hypot(projection(..))`, no decoder argument). A seed that consults anything
about the data's geometry would raise decoder quality at high K and let the support move fire
there too.

### Recommended shape for landing

- one guarded support move, attempted once the decoders have settled (the accepted moves here

  objective, so the alternation stays monotone and the existing recurrence certificate is

===== comment by SauersML at 2026-07-27T18:18:32Z =====
## A fourth routing defect: the greedy score and the acceptance test optimise different functionals

Three routing defects were already established here (hashed chart coordinate, marginal rather than residual-greedy selection, decoder-blind cold-start `score`). Re-reading `penalized_objective` against `reroute_fixed_decoder` turns up a fourth, and it is the one that explains the guard telemetry.

### The mismatch

`penalized_objective` charges a coordinate prior on every occupied slot (`support_term.rs`, the `ArdAxisPrior::eval` term in the per-row sum):

```rust
row_value += ArdAxisPrior::eval(
    ard_precisions[atom][axis],
    self.assignment.coords_for_slot(row, slot)[axis],
    periods[axis],
).value;
```

which for a Euclidean axis is `0.5·α·t²`.

`reroute_fixed_decoder` scores candidates by the fit term only:

```rust
let score = row.iter().zip(decoded.iter())
    .map(|(truth, fit)| 2.0 * truth * fit - fit * fit)
    .sum::<f64>();
```

Differencing the objective properly: adding atom `k` to a row at chart coordinate `t` moves `penalized_objective` by

```
Δ = −0.5·(2·rᵀγ_k(t) − ‖γ_k(t)‖²) + Σ_axes V(α_k,axis, t_axis)
```

so the quantity a greedy step should **maximise** is `gain − 2·V`, not `gain`. Routing pushes `|t|` outward to buy fit; the prior charges for it; the guarded support move — which accepts only on a strict decrease of the full `penalized_objective` — then rejects the move that routing just proposed. The greedy step is adversarial to its own gate.

### Why this is the explanation for the guard telemetry, not a guess

Measured acceptance of the guarded support move (real Qwen3.5-4B L16, doc split, L0=8, fixed λ):

| K | support moves accepted | held-out EV |
|---|---|---|
| 2,000 | 1 of 3 (accept@26, reject@51,@76) | 0.8206 |
| 4,000 | 1 of 3 (accept@26, reject@51,@76) | 0.8290 |
| 8,000 | **0 of 3** (reject@26,@51,@76) | 0.8039 |

The missing term is charged on `n_rows × support_k × axes` slots, so its total grows with K while the per-atom fit gain does not. A score that omits it therefore degrades *exactly* as the model grows — which is the observed pattern, and it also accounts for K=8,000 scoring *below* K=4,000 rather than merely saturating.

The general lesson, which I think is worth stating because it is not specific to this lane: **when a proposal is screened by an acceptance test, the proposal's score must be obtained by differencing the acceptance objective**, not by reaching for the obvious fit term. A guard that rejects *more often as the model grows* is evidence that the proposal is scoring the wrong functional, not evidence that the model is saturating.

### Fix

Route against the same functional the guard evaluates. The prior is a property of the trial coordinate alone, so it precomputes per (atom, grid slot) and costs nothing in the inner loop:

```rust
let prior_term: Vec<f64> = (0..slots).map(|slot| {
    let atom_index = slot / omp_grid;
    2.0 * ArdAxisPrior::eval(
        ard_precisions[atom_index][0],
        theta[slot],
        self.atom_axis_periods(atom_index)[0],
    ).value
}).collect();
...
let gain = 2.0 * cross - self_term[slot] - prior_term[slot];
```

and the guard proposes with the precisions it is about to be judged under.

Currently rebuilding to measure whether this raises the K=8,000 acceptance rate. Numbers to follow either way — if acceptance stays at 0/3 the diagnosis above is wrong and I will say so.

### Also worth recording: the score reduces so `P` leaves the inner loop

With `c_k = B_k r` and `G_k = B_k B_kᵀ` (the latter row-independent, computed once per atom):

```
2·rᵀγ_k(t) − ‖γ_k(t)‖² = 2·φ(t)ᵀc_k − φ(t)ᵀG_kφ(t)
```

so evaluating a trial coordinate costs `O(m_k²)` in the basis width rather than `O(m_k·P)` in the ambient width. With `P=128` and `m_k≈8` that is the difference between a grid search being affordable and not.

## Landed on main as `41dde8941`

All four routing defects are fixed unconditionally — no env-var gating, and the two constants the experiment carried are gone:

- **grid resolution** → `atom.basis_size()`. A basis carrying `m` coefficients cannot resolve more than about `m` independent features along its chart, so `m` trial points is the basis's own resolution, not a tuning knob.
- **re-route cadence** → deleted. The support move is proposed **at the inner fixed point**, so convergence itself is the trigger.

That second change also strengthens the certificate. Previously `solve_fixed_point` certified a point that was stationary in the coordinates and decoders *at a frozen support*. It now certifies a point that is additionally **locally optimal against a support re-route** — the move is attempted exactly when the old certificate would have fired, and the certificate only stands if the move is rejected on a strict objective decrease.

Two things worth flagging from the implementation:

**The Anderson history must be dropped on an accepted move.** A support move changes the fixed-point map itself, so every stored difference describes a map that no longer exists. Feeding those secants to the extrapolator afterwards is fitting a model to a trajectory that never happened. `accelerator.reset()` + zeroed `taken_step` + `last_objective = None` on acceptance.

**A near-miss worth recording.** Removing the cadence block also removed `self.snapshot_coordinates(&mut cycle_start)` from the loop top, because the experiment had inserted the block immediately above it. That would have left `cycle_start` never refreshed and the Anderson residual taken against a stale iterate — wrong results, silently. It was caught only because the binding then read as an unused `mut`. Restored explicitly, with a comment saying why it sits there.

Multi-axis atoms still fall back to the hashed coordinate: a product grid is exponential in the latent dimension, and the overcomplete lane this serves admits 1-D charts. That is a real remaining limitation rather than a solved case, so it is stated in the code rather than papered over.

-------------------------------- ISSUE BODY ------------------------------------
The overcomplete support lane's wall-clock is **not** in the fixed point. It is in the LAML
criterion's stochastic trace estimator, which runs unpreconditioned CG that stagnates to its
iteration cap on every probe.

### Measured, with telemetry (`gamfit._rust.set_log_level("info")`)

N=5,000, K=500, `top_k=8`, real Qwen3.5-4B L16 PCA-128 chart, 14 threads:

```
seed:                    0.3 s
inner fixed point:       113 cycles, DONE at  [18s]
...then 6+ minutes with zero log output, burning 14 cores
```

One outer evaluation = one inner solve (**18 s**) + one LAML criterion (**> 6 min**). The
fixed point is ~5% of the cost. This is why the flagship shapes never returned: K=4,000/N=44,818
ran 47 min and K=2,000/N=44,818 ran 47 min, both without completing, and **a 20x reduction in
K changed nothing** — because this term does not shrink the way the decoder sweep does.

### The mechanism

`crates/gam-sae/src/manifold/support_outer.rs`:

```rust
const SUPPORT_LAML_TRACE_PROBES: usize = 16;
const SUPPORT_LAML_CG_REL_TOL: f64 = 1.0e-8;
...
let max_iters = beta_dim.saturating_mul(2).clamp(128, 4096);
for probe in 0..SUPPORT_LAML_TRACE_PROBES {
    ... matrix_free_arrow_inverse_apply(system, cache, rhs_t, rhs_beta,
                                        SUPPORT_LAML_CG_REL_TOL, max_iters)
}
```

At K=500, `beta_dim = K·m·p ≈ 500·3·128 = 192,000`, so `max_iters` saturates at **4096**.
Sixteen probes per smoothing group, three groups, 4096 CG iterations each, every one a matvec
over a 192,000-dimensional arrow system — order **4·10^11 flops per outer evaluation**, against
an 18-second fixed point.

**I tested the obvious knob and it is not the fix.** Rebuilding with
`SUPPORT_LAML_CG_REL_TOL = 1.0e-4` changed nothing — same 1 solve / 113 cycles then 260 s of
silence. That is the diagnostic: if loosening the tolerance by four orders of magnitude does
not shorten the solve, **CG is not converging to the tolerance at all — it is stagnating and
running to `max_iters`.** The arrow system is ill-conditioned and the solve is unpreconditioned.

### Also worth fixing on its own terms: the tolerance is mismatched to the estimator

Even if CG did converge, `1.0e-8` is the wrong target. A 16-probe Hutchinson estimator has a
standard error of order `1/sqrt(16) = 25%` on the trace it is estimating. Solving each probe
to 1e-8 buys precision that the estimator's own noise swamps by roughly **seven orders of
magnitude**. Whatever else changes, that constant should be derived from the probe count, not
picked independently of it.

### Suggested directions, cheapest first

1. **Precondition the CG.** The system is explicitly an *arrow* system; its block-diagonal
   part is exactly the cheap preconditioner that fixes stagnation, and the machinery to apply
   it already exists alongside `matrix_free_arrow_inverse_apply`.
2. **Tie the CG tolerance to the probe count** rather than hard-coding 1e-8 (SPEC-21: no
   arbitrary constants) — a Hutchinson estimate does not deserve a tolerance far below its own
   variance.
3. **Consider the exact log-determinant.** For an arrow/blocked system the determinant factors
   through a Schur complement, which may make the stochastic estimator unnecessary here
   entirely — and would remove both the probe noise and the 48 CG solves per evaluation.
4. **Emit something.** Six minutes of a single outer evaluation with no log line is what made
   this invisible; #2472 added per-evaluation progress but the expensive part is *inside* one
   evaluation.

Related: #2575 (I previously attributed the cost to the fixed point's linear rate and to
per-row allocation churn — both real but second-order next to this), #2502 (this is what is
blocking the flagship), #2573.

measured 1.4e-3 resolution". Both halves were wrong: `sqrt(2/32)/32` is 7.8e-3,

the code actually installed violated the requirement its own doc comment stated.

The error is not arithmetic, it is directional. The requirement is
`m*delta <~ sigma/sqrt(m)`: the bias, which the average does not shrink because
it is identical in every term, must stay under the stochastic error, which the
average does shrink. Substituting the a-priori worst case for `sigma` moves the
bound the WRONG WAY — it is an upper bound on sigma, so it yields an upper bound
on the ALLOWED delta, while a safe working value needs a LOWER bound on sigma.

is 1.9e-3 at 16 probes, two orders under the `sqrt(2/m)` bound. No a-priori
constant can be safe for this.

number it measures: a quadrature bias is common to every probe and cancels out of
the across-probe spread `std_err` is. The pilot's shifted solves do run at the
working `cg_rel_tol`, since per-probe solve jitter would not cancel.

The working budget is `sigma_hat/m`, clamped to [shared rel_tol, bound] so it is
never tighter than the shared policy would have asked nor looser than the worst

SAE border is O(n*K) and is where the reported six minutes of silent burn
actually goes (measured here: 11.6 s of "factorization" against 8.4 s of inner

* `factor_arrow_evidence_rows(sys, options)` does exactly the row-side work —

> Materialize the b needed cross-block columns through the ROUTED `H_tβ`
> convention (`sys_htbeta_apply_row` …) at the row's OWN width `di` — never a
> raw `row.htbeta` read at the global `sys.d`: matvec-backed rows carry
> absent/zero-sized slabs by contract (a raw read is wrong or panics)

`build_schur_scalar_inv` repeats it; `row_hessian_fingerprint_for_system`
handles it. The coupling-graph builder was the one place on the ladder that was
never converted, and it is the FIRST thing every escalated tier constructs.

## The fix

`BetaCouplingGraph::build_from_system(sys)`. Each row's cross block is recovered
EXACTLY through the routed transpose — `H_βt^(i) e_c` is row `c` of
`H_tβ^(i)` — so `d_i` operator applications per row, with the per-column
activity accumulated in ABSOLUTE value: a column that is nonzero in two latent
rows and cancels across them still reads as active, which is what the element
scan tested and what an unsigned ones-probe would miss. For a dense system the
routed transpose returns the slab's own entries, so the decisions are identical.

Taking the system also deletes the clone: the old signature copied every row's
`(d, k)` block, `O(n·d·k)` of transient memory, once per preconditioner build.

Cost note in the doc: the scan it replaces exited early per block; the new pass
is one linear sweep over the cross-block data (`Σ_i d_i·k`), which for a dense
system is a sweep over slabs the system already holds resident.

TITLE : curvature_admissible() passes vacuously when curvature was never measured: psd()==None satisfies the gate, and the support-sparse LAML lane reports hessian=Unavailable so it can never fail there
STATE : OPEN
AUTHOR: SauersML
OPENED: 2026-07-27T16:32:42Z
UPDATED: 2026-07-27T18:33:48Z
URL   : https://github.com/SauersML/gam/issues/2578
LABELS: 

-------------------------------- ISSUE BODY ------------------------------------
`curvature_admissible()` returns `true` when curvature was never measured, so on any lane
whose Hessian is unavailable the check passes vacuously — it can never fail, and its `true`
carries no information.

`crates/gam-solve/src/model_types/result_types.rs:1134`:

```rust
pub fn curvature_admissible(&self) -> bool {
    // The raw measurement decides it whenever it is not a refusal. Only a
    // measured `false` consults the floor, and then only to ask whether
    // that negative direction was distinguishable from zero at all.
    self.curvature.psd() != Some(false)
        || self.curvature_floor.is_some_and(|clearance| clearance.cleared)
}
```

The comment describes a three-state reading — measured-true, measured-false, refusal — but the
expression collapses two of them. `psd()` is an `Option<bool>`, and:

| `psd()` | meaning | `!= Some(false)` | admissible? |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Some(true)` | measured PSD | true | yes — correct |
| `Some(false)` | measured indefinite | false → consults floor | correct |
| **`None`** | **never measured** | **true** | **yes — vacuous** |

So "we did not look" and "we looked and it was fine" produce the same verdict. That is the
absence-satisfies-a-conjunct shape: a gate whose passing condition is met by the *lack* of an
observation cannot fail on the case it exists to catch.

### This is not hypothetical for the overcomplete SAE lane

The support-sparse grouped-LAML outer loop reports its own curvature as unavailable — from its
startup line, verbatim:

```
[OUTER] support-sparse TopK grouped LAML: n_params=3, gradient=Analytic,
hessian=Unavailable -> solver=Bfgs, hessian_source=BfgsApprox
[solver=Bfgs;hessian=BfgsApprox;matrix-free=false] [no Hessian: BFGS approximation]
```

`hessian=Unavailable` means there is no measured curvature to be `Some(_)`, so every
`curvature_admissible()` consulted on this lane answers `true` without inspecting anything.
Whatever that predicate protects is unprotected here.

### Suggested shape

Make the missing case explicit rather than folding it into the pass branch — either
`Some(true) => admissible`, `Some(false) => consult floor`, `None => not admissible` (or a
distinct `Unknown` verdict that callers must handle), so a lane that cannot measure curvature
is forced to say so instead of silently inheriting a pass. If some callers legitimately want
"do not block on unmeasured curvature", that should be their explicit choice at the call site,
not a default buried in the predicate.

Found while auditing the overcomplete lane's certificate machinery (gam#2502 / #2577); the
adjacent observation, that `max_change` saturates at exactly the trust radius, turns out NOT to
be a gate — `solve_fixed_point` accepts on objective recurrence and only *reports*
`max_recurrence_change` — so that one is a misleading diagnostic rather than a broken check.
Sweeping `trust_radius` over {1, 4, 16, 64} gives identical objective trajectories, confirming
the saturating quantity is a gauge direction with no effect on the fit.

TITLE : Overcomplete (K>P) manifold SAE serializes but cannot be reloaded: two Rust types share the name ManifoldSAE, the loader sniff accepts a schema its parser rejects
STATE : OPEN
AUTHOR: SauersML
OPENED: 2026-07-27T02:25:45Z
UPDATED: 2026-07-27T05:41:50Z
URL   : https://github.com/SauersML/gam/issues/2567
LABELS: 

-------------------------------- ISSUE BODY ------------------------------------
The overcomplete (`K > P`) support-sparse manifold SAE is the only lane that can fit a
`K ≫ P` TopK curved dictionary, and it is the one lane whose fit **cannot be reloaded**.
It serializes, `gamfit.load` claims the file, and the parser then rejects it. SPEC-20
requires "Work survives walls via checkpoint/resume"; for this lane there is no resume.

### The three facts, at `300881ba1`

1. **Two distinct Rust types share one public Python name.**
   - `crates/gam-pyffi/src/manifold/manifold_and_posterior_ffi.rs:6780` —
     `ManifoldSaeCore`, `#[pyclass(name = "ManifoldSAE")]`, dense lane, has `from_dict`.
   - `crates/gam-pyffi/src/manifold/support_sparse_sae_ffi.rs:35` —
     `SupportSparseManifoldSaeCore`, also `#[pyclass(name = "ManifoldSAE")]`, has
     `to_dict` and **no** `from_dict`.

   Only `ManifoldSaeCore` is ever registered (`geometry_ffi.rs:4916`,
   `module.add_class::<ManifoldSaeCore>()`). `SupportSparseManifoldSaeCore` has no
   `add_class` anywhere — it only escapes as a return value from
   `fit_support_sparse_manifold_sae`. So `gamfit.ManifoldSAE` *is* the dense class,
   while a `K > P` fit returns an instance of a different, unregistered type that
   reports the same `__name__`. `isinstance(fit, gamfit.ManifoldSAE)` is therefore
   `False` for the overcomplete lane.

2. **The sniff accepts what the parser refuses.** `gamfit/_api.py:1373` routes on a
   substring:
   ```python
   if head.startswith(b"{") and b"gamfit.ManifoldSAE" in raw[:512]:
       return ManifoldSAE.from_dict(json.loads(raw.decode("utf-8")))
   ```
   Support payloads are tagged `gamfit.ManifoldSAE/support-v1`
   (`support_sparse_sae_ffi.rs:297`), which **contains** that substring, so they pass
   the sniff. `manifold_sae_payload.rs:37` then pins `SCHEMA_TAG =
   "gamfit.ManifoldSAE/v6"` and documents that `from_json` "rejects any other value,
   matching the Python `from_dict` guard". The load path is wired to claim the file and
   then hard-fail on it.

3. **A live consumer is already broken by this.** `splice_eval.py` (the ΔCE splice
   benchmark for #2502) does `gamfit.ManifoldSAE.from_dict(pickle.load(f))` on a
   support payload. It cannot ever have worked against a `K > P` fit.

### Why it matters beyond a missing method

Because there is no reload, every downstream analysis of an overcomplete dictionary —
splice ΔCE, steering, interpretation, any re-scoring — must run *inside the fitting
process*. That is not a style problem, it is a durability problem: on 2026-07-26 the
#2502 K=32,000 flagship died with its session at 22:10Z after the fit had already been
launched, and nothing was recoverable because nothing could be written and reopened. The
lane's own error messages are careful about non-convergence (SPEC-20 first sentence) while
the second sentence of the same rule — checkpoint/resume — has no implementation here.

### Proposed fix (root cause, not the symptom)

The symptom fix is "add `from_dict` to the support class". The root cause is that one
public name is doing duty for two types with two schemas and no dispatch, so:

1. Give the support payload a real inverse: a `from_dict` on the support class that
   round-trips `support-v1`. `to_dict` already emits everything structurally required —
   `retained_atom_indices`, `atom_basis`, `atom_dim`, `top_k`, `training_mean`,
   `decoder_blocks`, `support_indices`, `support_values`, `coords` — so the term and its
   assignment state can be rebuilt without re-fitting.
2. Make the dispatch explicit rather than substring-based: route on the **exact** schema
   tag, sending `/v6` to the dense class and `/support-v1` to the support class, and
   raise a typed error naming both for anything else.
3. Decide the name collision deliberately: either register the support class under its
   own Python name, or unify the two behind one type with a lane discriminator. Two
   unregistered-vs-registered classes silently sharing `__name__` is the thing that makes
   (1) and (2) look like they already work.

Acceptance: fit a `K > P` dictionary, `to_dict` → JSON → `gamfit.load` → `reconstruct`
reproduces the in-process `reconstruct` bitwise; `isinstance` is true for whichever class
the loader returns; and `splice_eval.py` runs against a saved flagship without change.
